Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 31, 2026 · Applies to: Sneaklings — Paint Hide & Seek and other Balagan Games titles

The short version: Sneaklings has no user accounts and no ads. It sends anonymous game statistics and crash reports so we can fix bugs and make the game better; you can turn this off in Settings (ANALYTICS). We do not run our own servers and do not sell your personal data. The only information we look at is what a player report contains, and we use it only to keep the game safe. Online play and purchases are handled by the platform services described below. Playing online also means agreeing to our Terms of Use.

Who we are

Balagan Games is the studio name of Etay Matzliah, an independent game developer registered in Israel. You can reach us at support@balagangames.com.

What we do NOT do

  • We do not require or offer user accounts, registration, or sign-in.
  • We do not show advertisements.
  • We do not track you across other apps or websites, and we do not use advertising identifiers.
  • We do not sell, rent, or share personal data with third parties for marketing.
  • We do not operate servers that store your data.

Online multiplayer (Epic Online Services)

Sneaklings uses Epic Online Services (EOS), operated by Epic Games, Inc., for lobbies, matchmaking, and connecting players. To make this work:

  • The game creates an anonymous device identifier on your device. It is not linked to your name, email, or any account, and is used only to keep you connected to game sessions.
  • Your IP address is processed transiently by Epic's infrastructure to route game traffic between players. We never see or store it.
  • The game also creates a random player ID on your device. It is shared with the other players' devices in your room so that you can rejoin after a dropped connection and so that a removed player can't come back in. It is not linked to your name, email, or any account.
  • So that bans and blocks keep working after a reinstall, the game also makes a device code: a one-way scrambled form of your device's own identifier (on Android, the app's Android ID; on a computer, the machine ID). The identifier itself never leaves your device, and the code can't be turned back into it. The device code is sent to the host of a room you join, travels in a report about you, and can appear on the public ban list if you are banned.
  • The nickname you enter and your in-game actions (such as paint strokes) are visible to the other players in your session while you play. They are not stored after the session ends, except inside a player report (below) and in a clip or picture another player chooses to share (see "Clips and pictures players share").

Epic Games processes this data under its own privacy policy: Epic Games Privacy Policy.

Clips and pictures players share

At the end of a round, a player can turn a hiding spot from the gallery into a short clip or a picture and share it. What you painted on your Sneakling can appear in it, so clips and pictures other players make can show your paint, but never your nickname, the room code, or any chat. Nothing leaves a device unless the player who made it chooses to share it: the game does not upload clips or pictures anywhere, it hands the file to the share sheet of the player's own device (or saves a picture to their computer).

Player reports, blocking, and bans

To keep rooms safe, players can report each other from inside the game (see our Terms of Use). When you send a report, the game sends it to Epic's player reports service, part of Epic Online Services, where we review it. A report contains:

  • the reason you picked;
  • the anonymous Epic identifiers of you and of the reported player for that session;
  • the reported player's nickname and ban code (a one-way scrambled form of their random player ID; it can't be turned back into the ID), and their device code when the room's host knows it;
  • the game mode, room code, and round;
  • possibly a small picture of the reported player's in-game paint, so we can see what was reported.

The reported player is never told who reported them. We use reports only to moderate the game and never share them with anyone else. Reports stay in Epic's service, under Epic's privacy policy; we keep no copies of our own.

If a player is banned, their ban code (and their device code, when the report has one) is added to a list published on this website, which the game checks before joining public rooms. A ban can be temporary or permanent. The list contains these codes and, for a temporary ban, the date and time it ends: no names and no other information.

If you block a player, the game remembers their ban code and device code on your device only, so you aren't matched with them again. When you join a public room, your block list is shared with that room's host so the game can keep you apart. The game also remembers on your device that you agreed to the Terms of Use.

Game statistics and crash reports

To find bugs and learn what to improve, the game sends us anonymous statistics about how it is played, and a report when it crashes or hits an error.

  • An anonymous install ID. The first time you open the game it makes a random ID and keeps it on your device. It is not made from your device's hardware, and it is not linked to your name, email, nickname, or any account. Deleting the game's data makes a new one (on a phone, uninstalling the game does that).
  • What the statistics contain, for example: your platform, game version and which store the game came from; whether you own the Full Game; how many days since you installed, how many times you have opened the game and how many matches you have played; the region part of your device's language setting (for example "IL" in "he_IL"); your settings (sound, music, name tags, emotes, vibration, full screen, key hints); which screens and features you use (for example the tutorial, the Look Book, emotes); and short summaries of each room and round: how you joined (code, QR, Quick Play, invite link, or installing the game through an invite link), why you left (for example you left, the host ended it, the connection dropped, you were kicked, voted out or banned), how many players, reports, blocks and kick votes the room had, and for each round the map, mode, your role, the shape you wore, your score and how long it lasted. If you buy the Full Game, we see that a purchase happened, never your payment details.
  • What they never contain: your nickname, your paint, room codes, your Epic or Steam identifiers, the device code, advertising IDs, or anything you type.
  • No location from your connection. Your IP address reaches these services as part of any internet connection, but neither stores it for us and neither works out a location from it. The only regional detail is your device's language region, above.
  • Crash and error reports contain what went wrong (the error, where in the game's code it happened, the game's last few warnings, and for a hard crash a technical snapshot of the game's state at that moment), your device model, operating system and game version, and the same anonymous install ID (a hard crash may also carry Sentry's own random ID for the installation). Folder names that could contain your computer's user name are removed before a report is stored. Each time the game starts, Sentry also gets a short session note: when the game started and stopped, whether it crashed, and the install ID. That is how we count how often each version crashes.
  • Who processes it: statistics go to PostHog (PostHog, Inc.) and crash reports to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), both on their servers in the European Union, acting on our behalf under their own privacy policies (posthog.com/privacy, sentry.io/privacy).
  • Turning it off: Settings → ANALYTICS. It is on by default. Off stops the statistics and the crash reports straight away and deletes statistics not yet sent. A crash report already on its way at that moment may still arrive.

We use this data only to improve the game. We never sell it, never use it for ads, and never combine it with data from other apps.

Purchases

In-app purchases and paid downloads are processed entirely by the platform store you use — Google Play (Google), the App Store (Apple), or Steam (Valve). We never receive your payment details. Refunds and payment questions are handled by the respective store under its own policies.

Children

Sneaklings is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Online sessions can include other players, so we recommend that younger teens play with people they know.

Data security and retention

We do not store personal data on servers of our own. Player reports are kept in Epic's service only as long as we need them to moderate. Ban codes and device codes stay on the ban list for as long as the ban lasts. Game statistics are kept for up to one year. Crash reports are deleted automatically after 30 days. Data processed by Epic Games or the platform stores is governed by their policies, linked above.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or similar laws), you may have rights to access, correct, or delete personal data. For reports or bans that concern you, email us. Requests concerning other multiplayer or purchase data should be directed to Epic Games or your platform store. If you are unsure, email us and we will point you the right way. Because the game statistics and crash reports are anonymous, we usually cannot tell which ones are yours; you can stop them at any time with the ANALYTICS setting.

Changes to this policy

If we ever change how the game handles data (for example, adding a new online feature), we will update this page and change the effective date above.
